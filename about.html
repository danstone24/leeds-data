About this site

Leeds Data turns the open data that Leeds City Council publishes into charts anyone can read, without opening a spreadsheet.

Why it exists

The council publishes hundreds of datasets at Datamillnorth.org, on potholes, spending, road safety, traffic, footfall and more. Most of it is raw CSV. I built this site so anyone can see how Leeds is doing without opening a spreadsheet.

Every chart answers a plain question: are our roads getting safer? how fast are potholes fixed? do you still need a car?

Where the numbers come from

Almost everything here comes straight from Leeds City Council's own published data. Three topics cover things the council doesn't publish, so they use official national statistics instead: air quality (DEFRA's monitoring network), recycling & waste (DEFRA's local-authority returns) and planning (MHCLG's quarterly statistics, plus a map layer from the volunteer-run PlanIt). I don't add my own estimates or opinions to the figures. Each topic page links back to the exact dataset it uses, and shows when it was last updated, so you can always check the source yourself. The data is published under the Open Government Licence.

The site checks for new data every night, so the charts stay current as fresh figures are published, usually monthly, quarterly or annually, depending on the dataset.

How I handle the numbers

Council data is often messy: files change format over the years, labels are spelled inconsistently, and some records are incomplete. I clean and standardise where it's clearly safe to, and say so where it isn't. Two rules apply throughout:

  • Show what the council published. Where records are missing or unclear, the page says so rather than filling gaps with guesses.
  • Compare like with like. When the number of sensors or cameras behind a dataset changes over time, raw totals mislead, so the charts average per sensor instead.

How each dataset is treated

One line on the main quirk behind each page. The full notes sit under each page's charts.

  • Council spending

    Most rows leave the department blank, so it is inferred from the service area. Supplier names are not deduplicated.

  • Potholes & road repairs

    One row per recorded pothole. "Time to fix" excludes potholes still awaiting repair.

  • Road safety & collisions

    One row per injured casualty, so a single crash can produce several. Unreported injuries never appear.

  • Getting around (cycling & traffic)

    Average count per recorder per day, not raw totals, because the set of working recorders changes month to month.

  • City centre footfall

    Overlapping files are merged and de-duplicated per hour, then averaged per camera for the same reason.

  • Council tax

    Cash amounts, headline charge before discounts. The adult social care precept is folded into the council's share.

  • Council housing

    Bids measure competition, not the waiting list. By-ward figures use the post-2018 wards only.

  • School places

    Places offered and filled are only published for council-run schools, so those charts are mostly primaries.

  • Air quality

    DEFRA's Leeds Centre background monitor. Years under 75% coverage are withheld and the latest readings are provisional.

  • Recycling & waste

    DEFRA statistics for the whole council area. Fly-tipping counts partly reflect how incidents are reported.

  • Planning applications

    MHCLG quarterly returns for every chart. The map comes from PlanIt and is a guide, not statistics.

How the site is built

Leeds Data is a set of plain web pages on a content network, plus a nightly job that downloads any new data, aggregates it, and stores summaries for the pages to load. No tracking, no adverts. It is open source, including the exact steps used to process each dataset, on GitHub.
